# The 5-Day Focus Reset

A simple system to reclaim your attention and get more done in less time.

Most productivity advice fails because it asks you to become a different person
overnight. This book asks for five days and small changes. That's it.

## Why Your Focus Feels Broken

You are not lazy and you are not broken. Your attention is being harvested by
systems designed to keep you scrolling. Understanding this is the first step to
taking it back.

Every notification is a tiny slot machine. Every open tab is an unfinished
loop your brain keeps returning to. The result is a day that feels busy but
produces little.

- Constant context-switching costs you up to 20 minutes of refocus each time.
- Willpower is not the answer — environment design is.
- Small, boring habits beat big, dramatic overhauls.

> The goal is not to work more hours. It is to make the hours you work count.

### Your next step
Write down the three things that most reliably break your focus. Awareness is
leverage.

## Day 1: Clear the Deck

Before you add anything, remove friction. Today you strip your environment down
to what actually matters.

Turn off all non-human notifications. Close every tab that isn't part of today's
work. Put your phone in another room during your first deep-work block.

### Your next step
Do one 45-minute focus block today with your phone in another room. Notice how
it feels.

## Day 2: The One-Thing Morning

Multitasking is a myth. Today you learn to start the day with a single, clear
priority and protect it.

Each morning, before email, write the one task that would make today a win.
Do it first, before the world wakes up and starts making requests of you.

- Pick the task the night before so mornings have no friction.
- Protect the first 90 minutes like a meeting with your future self.

### Your next step
Choose tomorrow's One Thing tonight. Put it on a sticky note where you'll see it.

## Day 3: Design Your Environment

Motivation is unreliable. Environment is not. Today you make focus the path of
least resistance.

Keep your desk clear. Keep distracting apps behind logins you have to type.
Make the good choice the easy choice and the bad choice slightly annoying.

### Your next step
Remove one source of friction from your workspace and add one to a distraction.

## Day 4: Batch and Protect

Attention has switching costs. Today you group similar work and defend blocks
of time so your brain stops paying the switching tax.

Batch your email into two windows. Batch your meetings into an afternoon.
Guard your mornings for the deep work only you can do.

### Your next step
Block one two-hour "no meetings" window on your calendar for tomorrow.

## Day 5: Make It Stick

A five-day reset is worthless if it evaporates on day six. Today you turn the
experiment into a default.

Keep the habits that worked. Drop the ones that didn't. Write a one-line rule
for each keeper so future-you doesn't have to decide again.

> Discipline is just a decision you only have to make once.

### Your next step
Write your three focus rules on one card. Read it every morning for a week.

## Where to Go From Here

You now have a working system. The reset was never about five perfect days —
it was about proving to yourself that your attention is trainable.

Repeat the reset any time life knocks you off track. It works because it is
small enough to actually do.
