The look: observed, not imagined. Each frame should feel like it was filmed by a real camera that happened to be there: an orbital camera, a drone, a tripod on a ridge. No fantasy colours, no spinning camera.

Camera and light rules (every Seedance prompt)

  • Steady, deliberate camera. Locked tripod or slow dolly/drone moves. No barrel rolls, no orbiting during an impact. (In the comments, viewers hated restless cameras.)
  • Lock on the impact point at the moment of impact; never cut away or zoom out on the hit.
  • One hard light source per scene: sun at 5600 K in space, 4000 K overcast dawn on the ground, 3200 K warm tungsten for night interiors.
  • Physical scale from human references: houses, roads, stadiums, the Eiffel Tower.
  • Space scenes carry score and low designed sound only, never a literal "boom in vacuum".

Motion graphics system

  • 1 px cyan lines, monospace numbers, sentence-case labels.
  • Every number shows estimate or its source chip.
  • "SIMULATION" tag top-left on every illustrated event.
  • Meters and feet together on screen.
  • Graphics draw on in about 12 frames and hold until they're read.

Captions

  • Burned in, max 2 lines, about 32 characters per line, lower-centre with a soft scrim.
  • Timed to the final narration take (authored words, not auto-transcription).
  • Only the key number or comparison is highlighted in amber, never every word.
  • Placed clear of the graphic zone and YouTube controls.

Sound and music palette

  • Narrator: one calm, curious documentary voice. You pick it from the voice list before narration is generated.
  • Music: one original instrumental, escalating consequence → release. Low warm synth and soft piano pulse, then tense strings and sub pulse during the impact, thinning to a hopeful resolve for the outro. No vocals.
  • SFX: recorded with each Seedance clip (native audio, at no extra credit cost): atmospheric roar, delayed ground rumble, air-blast whoosh, water hiss, night crickets. Then mixed under the voice.
  • Silence is a tool: about 1 s of near-silence right after the flash.
